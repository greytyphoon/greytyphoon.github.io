Pathfinder 2nd-edition Houserules

Common Rules

  • All characters have equal XP.
  • All characters use Automatic Bonus Progression.
  • All characters gain a Free Archetype.
  • All characters gain one bonus skill increase at 3rd, 7th, 15th level.
  • When something makes you Stride, you can instead use any 1-action activity with the [Move] trait. For example, you could use Sudden Charge to Stand and Leap; follow up a Shove with Fly; use haste to Tumble Through with a jump spell.

Rarity

When using elements that are not [Common], you should check with the GM.

  • [Uncommon] elements are generally allowed. Please use them with some moderation.
  • [Rare] elements are generally restricted to one per character.
  • [Unique] elements generally should be obtained in-game.

Allowed Material

  • Official Paizo content is always allowed.
  • Third-party content and homebrews are generally allowed, but you should check with the GM.
  • Both Remastered and Legacy content are allowed. A character could even learn both ray of frost and frostbite. If unspecified, Remastered is assumed.

Estimating Enemy HP
  • You can know just by looking which enemies are Untouched (100%), Bloodied (≤50%), or Near Death (≤20%).
Take-backs or Retcons
  • As a rule of thumb, actions can be walked back as long as no dice roll gets overturned.
Dying for NPCs
  • When brought to 0 HP, unless by nonlethal means, NPCs and monsters become dying like PCs.
  • To streamline the game, they will always fail their recovery check.
  • As usual, some monsters like constructs and undeads are destroyed right away.
Dying for PCs
  • A dying character still has some agency.
  • While they're mechanically unconscious, they flicker in and out of consciousness, barely able to move and speak a few words.
  • By straining, they can take a single action on their turn. This adds 1 to their Dying value, and must be done before making the recovery check.
Exploration and Tension
  • To keep a fair, random time pressure, exploration actions will cause tension dice to be added to the pool.
  • For each 10 minutes spent exploring, a D6 is added to the pool. Alice is using Treat Wounds on Bob while Carol tries to Identify an item they found: only one dice is added to the pool.
  • Whenever someone takes a risk or does something unsafe, the pool is rolled: if any dice rolls a 1, something bad happens. While his party rests, Dan walks back to the previous room, alone, to ensure no loot was missed.
  • The pool is also rolled, then emptied, if it ever reaches its maximum of 6 dice.
Recall Knowledge
  • When you recall knowledge about a foe and succeed, I will provide an information I think is interesting on top of answering a question thruthfully. For an Otyugh, I might specify that its Stench can be deactivated by cleaning it thorougly.
  • When you recall knowledge about a foe and crit, I will usually give access to its full statblock.
  • Commonly accepted questions include:
    • What are its weaknesses, resistances, immunities? I might only give part of the list.
    • What is its weakest save?
    • Does it have a reaction? I might only reveal its most common reaction.
    • What conditions can it inflict? I might only reveal its most commonly inflicted condition. I will reveal how they are inflicted: via attacks, an aura, etc.
Player-vs-Player Interactions (PvP)
  • Usually, when PvP happens, players roll as usual, and the defender gets to change the degree of success by one step in either direction. Alice tries to Lie to Bob. Alice rolls Deception (a success!) but Bob changes it to failure, and is not fooled. Alice casts breathe fire on Bob. Bob rolls Reflex (a success!) but changes it to a crit, and takes no damage. Alice casts breathe fire on Bob. Bob rolls Reflex (a failure!) but changes it to a success. Bob has Evasive Reflexes, so it becomes a crit, and Bob takes no damage. Alice tries to Shove Bob away from a foe. Alice rolls Athletics (a failure!), but Bob changes it to a success, and moves away from the threat.
  • This rule does not apply if PvP is unintentional or unfortunate. For example, Alice casts breathe fire on a pack of zombies, and Bob is caught in the blast.
  • This rule is adapted from the sidebar on Gliminals, a monster forcing you to damage your allies to prevent death by over-healing.
Crafting Materials
  • If you're crafting items, learning spells, or casting rituals, you will often need specific magic supplies, worth a certain amount of gold.
  • When you're in town shopping, I will allow to convert gold into unspecified materials, to be tracked by value like gold. Alice has 500gp to spend. She decides to buy a Librarian Staff for 225gp and converts 250gp to materials. She has 25gp remaining..
  • Unspecified materials can be used for any purpose. We'll assume your character has planned ahead. Alice spends 36gp to Learn a Spell of 4th rank, as planned, then spends 200gp to reincarnate Bob. She has 14gp of materials remaining. She still has 25gp, but can't learn another spell.
  • Unspecified materials can be turned back into gold, but at half the price.
Illusions and Disbelieving
  • Many illusion spells mention that a creature must spend actions to disbelieve it.
  • However, this forces a bit of metagaming: why would a creature attempt to disbelieve, if they believe the illusion?
  • To simplify this, creatures will interact with each illusion on three degrees of success: Fooled, Wary, Aware.
    • Fooled creatures fully believe the illusion is real. When something irrealistic happens, they become Wary. For example, a clean hit with a sword sliced straight through.
    • Wary creatures think trickery is afoot. They can take an action to disbelieve. If they do and succeed, they become Aware.
    • Aware creatures fully disbelieve the illusion. At this stage, they can choose to ignore the illusion and see through it, or not, as naturally as focusing on a window or the object behind it.
  • When an illusion spell is cast, all who are affected make a Will (if the illusion is Mental) or Perception check (otherwise) against the caster's spell DC: those who succeed become Wary, those who fail are Fooled.
Line of Effect and the Manton Effect
  • I cast create water inside the foe's lungs, so they choke to death.
  • First, most spells' wording make this impossible. For example, telekinetic hand targets an unattended object, and your foe's aorta isn't one.
  • Second, you need line of effect, as specified in the rules, to do that. You don't have line of effect to your foe's inner ear.
  • Third, in case of ambiguity, I'll use the Manton Effect, from Worm. It divides powers between those that only affect organic matter, and those that only affect inorganic matter, with few exceptions. This prevents powers like fire generation from cooking foes from the inside. The Manton Effect exists in-game and magic scholars have researched it: any wizard worth their salt knows about it by name.

Optional Rules - Not used unless specifically mentioned

  1. Dual-Class PCs Gestalt

    A classic very-high-power game. Most likely used at low player counts.
    Note that because Dual-Class characters already get two class feats every even level, this rule generally replaces Free Archetype.

  2. Pushed Rolls from Call of Cthulhu

    Outside of combat, when you fail at a roll, you can decide to push your luck. You must describe another way that you retry, giving it all you have. This allows you to reroll the initial failed roll. However, should you fail again, you automatically get a critical failure. You cannot push a roll again until another player pushes a roll.

  3. Connections from Traveller RPG

    A character can become Trained in a bonus skill at 1st level. It must be a skill that is related to, or used in, a shared history that this character has with another player character in the same game. Work with other players to create those stories, which happened off-screen, usually before the start of the campaign.
